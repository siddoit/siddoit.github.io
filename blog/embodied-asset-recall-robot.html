A Robot That Remembers Where You Left Your Wrench

Published: April 15, 2026 · Robotics

"Go fetch the 10mm spanner" is a sentence no robot takes seriously — until now. Our final-year project is a robot that actually remembers where things were last seen, walks over, and points.

The pitch is dead simple: an indoor asset-recall robot that runs fully offline on cheap edge hardware, understands the layout of a lab or workshop, and can later navigate back to find a tool that got moved. The demos we built toward are industrial tool recall, safety-item inspection, and — if all else fails — finding your lost stuff at home.

What makes it not-boring

Plenty of robots move and detect. Ours does something harder: it remembers. The differentiator we kept coming back to is what it does not need — no cloud, no LiDAR, no RFID tags. Just a Jetson-class board, some vision-language models, and a stubborn belief that a robot should find your things without phoning a data center.

How the memory works

It builds a persistent, open-vocabulary 3D scene graph of the space — objects labelled with models like YOLO-World, Florence-2, and CLIP, so you can ask for things in plain language. When something's been moved, a Bayesian belief model drives active search: it guesses where the item is most likely to be and goes there first, instead of sweeping the room blind. That's the trick that makes it feel less like a vacuum and more like it knows the place.

Why it could be a paper

The angle that makes supervisors happy is "low-cost offline semantic memory plus probabilistic active search," and it holds up as a system paper if we collect the experiments properly. For us, the appeal is simpler: a robot that doesn't need the internet to know where you left your wrench is a robot you'd actually let loose in a workshop.
