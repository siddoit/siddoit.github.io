People see a model hit 97% accuracy and think it was clean. It wasn't. Getting a tiny YOLO to read red and green lights on a rover was three phases of fighting the hardware, the software, and honestly, ourselves.

The goal was simple on paper: run YOLO26 Small on the LISA dataset, just red and green lights, for edge deployment on the's rover. The execution was anything but.

First, the software had to go

First problem was the machine tripping over its own math engine. PyTorch and CUDA were not on speaking terms. Nothing glamorous, just clearing out the junk that was quietly breaking everything.

Then came the VRAM reality check. "Illegal instruction" errors, memory overflows, the usual. Windows was also choking on background plotting, so we yanked all the visual plotting and locked down CPU workers to stop the GPU from panicking.

The funniest one: the rig was running a stupid overclock â€” +215 core, +415 mem â€” that was flipping bits and quietly destroying the compute graph. Dialed it back to a boring +25/+50 and suddenly the math stopped lying. Sometimes the fix is just "stop trying to be a hero."

We wrote a small script to sample a 300-image calibration set and slipped past the bottleneck.
