The Plant Doctor With a Bouncer at the Door

Published: March 12, 2026 · Computer Vision

Train a model to spot diseased leaves and it will, with total confidence, diagnose your car's steering wheel as powdery mildew. The funny failure turned out to be the most useful lesson in the whole project.

The first version was a small TensorFlow model, PeaDetect, that knew four states of a pea plant. It worked — until it didn't. Because it had to pick something every time, it spread its confidence across the four classes and called anything unfamiliar a disease. That's the closed-world trap: a model that only knows plants assumes everything is a plant.

Hire a bouncer

The fix was to stop trusting the doctor and add a bouncer. Before any diagnosis, a fast vision model in the cloud (Groq's Llama vision) takes one look and asks the only question that matters: "is this even a plant?" If not, the system stops there and tells you plainly. No fake medical report for your laptop.

When the doctor isn't sure

If the local model is hesitant — below 60% confidence — the image gets a second opinion from Google Gemini, which returns a clean, structured answer: the disease, its status, and three things you can do about it. Picking the right model took real digging: I hit Gemini's rate limits and a deprecated SDK, and even stood up a local model via Ollama so the whole thing could run with no internet at all. Every route got tested before one earned the job.

A web app that talks back

All of this sits behind a Flask server with a phone-friendly interface. The screen shifts green when a plant is healthy, red when it's not, grey when it's "not a plant." You can tap buttons or just say "Capture" and "Diagnose" — the browser's speech engine handles it, and reads the result back to you so you don't have to squint at a screen in the field.

And it waters the plant

The last piece is physical. An ESP32 reads soil moisture and temperature, and a relay switches a pump on and off. The trickiest bug there was input lag, solved by ditching blocking delay() calls for a non-blocking timer so the board could listen and sense at the same time. A small serial bridge lets a button on the web page literally turn the water on.

Why it's interesting

The headline isn't "AI detects disease." It's that a reliable system is mostly about knowing what your model can't do, and building guardrails for exactly that. A bouncer, a fallback, and a pump — that's what turns a demo into something you'd actually use in a garden.
