How I Accidentally Automated My Blog Posts

Published: July 17, 2026 · Tooling

I didn't set out to automate my blog. I set out to stop fighting my own messy notes. Somewhere along the way I found a tool and a model that do the fighting for me — and July is when that free ride ends.

Falling into OpenCode

I stumbled onto OpenCode, an agentic, multimodal coding agent that runs in the terminal. Not another chatbot you paste code into — something that actually reads your files, plans, and edits. For someone who lives in a folder of HTML, CSS, and half-finished projects, that's a different kind of help. It's the agentic, multimodal side that got me: I can throw in a wall of text, a screenshot, a vague idea, and it figures out the move.

The model doing the heavy lifting

Under the hood, the model that clicked for me is hy3 — fast, cheap, and genuinely good at exactly this: taking a scrambled brain-dump and turning it into something coherent. For my use case it's been one of the best-fitting models I've touched. The catch is the clock: hy3 goes away this July, moving to a paid version. That stings, because the free tier is what made experimenting feel weightless.

Enter Nemotron 3 Ultra

The upside is that there's a strong free alternative lined up: NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra. It's an open-weights model built for research, and it's one of the best free open-weight options out there right now for exactly this kind of work — summarising, restructuring, cleaning up prose without sandblasting the voice out of it. I've been pointed at it as the drop-in for when hy3 flips to paid, and from what I've run, it earns the spot.

What my "automation" actually is

It's not a pipeline or a cron job. It's embarrassingly simple: I type my mind into the agent — the raw thoughts, the swearing, the half-structured war stories — and it summarises and perfects them into something post-worthy. The projects you've been reading about here went through exactly that. I write like a human mid-rant; it hands back a draft that reads like a person who edited it. That's the whole trick, and it's why the blog finally has more than a "coming soon" page.

Why it's worth a post

Because the interesting part isn't the tool, it's the shift: writing used to be the bottleneck, and now it isn't. Free, open-weights models like Nemotron 3 Ultra are good enough that a student can keep a real blog without paying for it. When hy3 goes paid this month, I'll just be one model over — and the posts will keep coming.
